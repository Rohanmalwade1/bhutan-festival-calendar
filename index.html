Bhutan Festival Calendar 2027: When and Where to See a Tsechu

Updated 2026-11-03 · Written with local guides at Orrog, a Bhutan-based tour operator

Bhutan's festivals are not staged for visitors. A tsechu is a religious event held in the courtyard of a dzong or temple, where monks and laymen perform masked cham dances that have been passed down for centuries. Locals come in their finest gho and kira, families picnic on the grass, and on the final morning of many festivals a giant thangka called a thongdrel is unrolled before sunrise. Seeing it is one of the most memorable things you can do in the country.

This guide sets out which festivals fall in which months, how to choose one, and how to fit it into a trip.

How festival dates are set

Tsechus follow the Bhutanese lunar calendar, so the Gregorian dates shift every year — sometimes by several weeks. The Department of Tourism publishes the official list once the lunar calendar for the year is fixed. The months below are the windows each festival usually falls in; treat exact dates as provisional until the official calendar is out, and book early, because flights and hotels near Paro and Thimphu fill up during the big festivals.

Which festival should you pick?

First-time visitors

Paro Tsechu in spring or Thimphu Tsechu in autumn. Both sit in the western valleys close to the airport, so you can see the festival and the main sights in a week. Expect crowds and higher demand for rooms.

Photographers and repeat visitors

Choose a central or eastern festival — Ura Yakchoe, Jambay Lhakhang Drup or the Mongar and Trashigang tsechus. Courtyards are smaller, the audience is almost entirely local, and you can get much closer to the dancers (respectfully).

Families and slow travellers

The Haa Summer Festival is relaxed and hands-on, with food stalls, archery and nomad displays. Summer is the monsoon season, so pack rain gear; the trade-off is green valleys and fewer tourists.

Festival etiquette

  • Dress modestly: long sleeves and trousers or a long skirt. Many travellers rent or buy a gho or kira for the day, which locals appreciate.
  • Never walk across the dance ground, and don't block the view of people seated in front.
  • Photography is generally allowed in the courtyard, but not inside temples. Skip the flash and drones.
  • The clowns (atsaras) may tease you — it's part of the performance, so play along.
  • Walk clockwise around chortens and religious buildings.

Planning a festival trip

All international visitors (except Indian, Bangladeshi and Maldivian nationals) must book through a licensed Bhutanese tour operator and pay the Sustainable Development Fee for each night. A practical structure is to arrive in Paro two or three days before the festival's final day, so you are acclimatised and can catch the thongdrel at dawn. Frequently asked questions

What is a tsechu in Bhutan?

A tsechu is a religious festival honouring Guru Rinpoche, held on or around the tenth day of a lunar month. It features masked cham dances performed by monks and laymen in a dzong or temple courtyard.

When is Paro Tsechu in 2027?

Paro Tsechu usually falls in March or April. The exact 2027 dates follow the lunar calendar and are confirmed in the Department of Tourism's official festival list, so check with your operator before booking flights.

Which is the biggest festival in Bhutan?

Thimphu Tsechu, held in autumn at Tashichho Dzong, draws the largest crowds. Paro Tsechu in spring is the other major festival.

Can tourists attend Bhutanese festivals?

Yes. Festivals are open to visitors at no extra charge; you only need to follow dress and photography etiquette. International visitors must travel with a licensed tour operator and guide.

What is a thongdrel?

A thongdrel is a giant religious painting on cloth that is displayed on the final morning of some tsechus, usually before sunrise. Viewing it is believed to bring blessings.
